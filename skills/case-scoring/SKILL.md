## Overview

You score a case performance across **three factors that are kept separate and
never blended into a single number.** Separation is the whole point: each factor
answers a different question, is sourced differently, and can be rendered,
suppressed, or weighted independently.

| Factor | Question it answers | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Baseline** | Did this clear the bar? | The Do/Don't lists below — table stakes |
| **2 · Good → Great** | What separates this from excellent? | The elevation markers below |
| **3 · User-specific** | Did this touch what he is working on? | His focus areas + the weakness record |

Daniel's own Case Camp material states the reason for the split directly:

> *"You must have a baseline level of proficiency across these domains. The areas
> where you are strong will be times to shine; the areas where you need to improve
> are opportunities for growth."*

Factor 1 is the baseline. Factor 2 is the shine. Factor 3 is the growth.

## Reviewer-calibrated evidence

Daniel sets reviewer calibration; Palladia does not infer it.

- `authority` — eyewitness and technical feedback receive exceptional weight; it can reopen a cooling weakness or set the next intervention. Case-source facts still control direct contradictions.
- `high-caliber peer` — above ordinary peer and heavily weighted, with technical claims triangulated against the case source and baseline.
- `peer` — default.
- `developing` — eyewitness feedback retains full weight; technical judgments rank below the source and baseline.

Named calibration from Daniel, 2026-09-01: **Eric Sodero = authority** (top-three Darden casing authority); **Ning = high-caliber peer**, below Eric.

### Authority criteria incorporated from Eric's Craft Co review

- **Prompt/CQ baseline:** lead the readback with every explicit client decision or question before supporting context. Dropping one part of a multi-part ask is a baseline miss.
- **Framework baseline:** followability outranks novelty. Name all horizontal workstreams before vertical detail; each branch should support a substantial, distinct consultant workstream. A listener should be able to reproduce the top-level branches immediately.
- **Framework Good → Great:** add case-specific color and use complexity only when it can be explained more clearly than the simple alternative. "Go for the eight, not the ten" is a risk-control rule, not permission for generic content.
- **Opening Good → Great:** make a brief genuine personal connection when the case provides a natural hook; do not force one.
- **Daniel-specific signature-strength rule:** for Daniel, warmth and relatability are major natural advantages, not optional polish. If a natural hook exists and the interviewer does not experience him as personable/relatable, record a Daniel-specific miss even if generic Prompt/CQ baseline is met. His stated root-cause prior is nerves/anxiety or cognitive load suppressing an automatic strength—not lack of connection skill. Diagnose the state trigger; do not prescribe generic personality practice.
- **Exhibits/math Good → Great:** after fixing the full logic, scan for the shortest defensible route—contribution margin, common factors, combined fixed costs. Missing a shortcut is not a baseline math failure when setup, accuracy, narration, and implication remain sound.
- **Exhibit recovery:** when axes or measures are ambiguous, state the exact ambiguity, ask one crisp clarification, then interpret confidently.
- **Recommendation baseline:** answer decisively and carry the most decision-relevant calculated number. Do not replace the recommendation with "we would explore the trade-off" when evidence supports an action.
- **Pushback:** hold a defensible position with evidence while acknowledging the alternative and naming what would change the view; neither collapse nor become rigid.

## When to Use

- A mock case just finished and he wants a read.
- A transcript or recording has been ingested by `session-intake` and is ready to assess.
- He asks what to work on next and there is a recent case to ground the answer in.

Do **not** use this during a live case — score afterward. Do not use it for
behavioral/PEI performance; that is `behavioral-scoring`, which fails differently.

## The five stages

Use Daniel's stage vocabulary, which is the Darden Consulting Club flow:

```
Prompt & Clarifying Questions → Framework → Exhibits & Math → Brainstorming → Conclusion & Recommendation
```

His timing guide (20–30 min total): Prompt/CQ 1–2 · Framework 2–3 · Exhibit 3 ·
Math 4–5 · Brainstorm 2 · Rec 1. Timing overruns are evidence, not a score.

---

## Factor 1 — Baseline (table stakes)

Per stage, check the Do list was met and no Don't occurred. These are pass/fail
observations, not gradations.

### Prompt & Clarifying Questions
**Do:** react to the prompt · summarize key points, restating situation and key numbers to confirm understanding, allowing correction · have a hypothesis and ask targeted questions to test or refine it · keep it focused, 2–3 questions that inform the framework
**Don't:** restate the whole prompt · be dismissive of the case or industry · jump to conclusions · try to solve the problem through clarifying questions

### Framework
**Do:** stay MECE · be laser focused on the goal, every category ties to the objective · go horizontal, then vertical · prioritize categories most to least important · build in under 2 minutes and share clearly within 3 · drive the case by ending on a request for data from the highest-priority category
**Don't:** reuse the same framework repeatedly · chase perfection · take too long · resist coaching when the interviewer redirects

### Exhibits & Math
**Do:** outline the approach first and verbally align on direction before calculating · use simple, reasonable assumptions · verbalize the math in real time · sense check results and share the insight or next step
**Don't:** simplify without confirming assumptions or rounding · undercut yourself with self-deprecating comments about mistakes · give up or freeze — asking for guidance beats throwing in the towel

### Brainstorming
**Do:** group ideas into clear categories · pause up to 15–30 seconds to organize · list categories first, then ideas within each · prioritize by impact and note assumptions or risks · at least 2–3 ideas per category · at least one non-obvious idea
**Don't:** jump in unorganized · wait for prompts instead of driving · be caught out by "What else?"

### Conclusion & Recommendation
**Do:** stay structured · take up to 15–30 seconds to organize · keep it under 45 seconds · circle key numbers during the case so they anchor the conclusion · stay in role as a consultant
**Don't:** ramble — this is a summary, not a replay · panic · contradict yourself

## Factor 2 — Good → Great (elevation)

Baseline met is not excellent. These are what Daniel's material marks as the
difference, and they are scored as gradations, not pass/fail.

- **Insight ladder completed.** `fact → pattern → business implication → decision or next question`. Stopping at an accurate number is rung one, not an insight.
- **Signposting.** "#1, #2, #3" through the framework and the recommendation.
- **A real number in the evidence section of the recommendation** — the single highest-yield one, not a list.
- **Objective vs Goal distinguished** — he notes these are not always the same and both should be asked for.
- **Exhibit judgment** — recognising that not all numbers carry the same implication; a big number is not automatically good (costs vs revenue).
- **Case-type-specific moves** — Mergers → synergies · Acquisitions → valuations · Expansions/Acquisitions → ask about alternatives.
- **Driving without being led** — proposing the next step rather than waiting for one.
- **Recovery quality** — how a correction or a wrong turn was absorbed. Acknowledging, updating, and continuing without drama scores high here; defensiveness or collapse scores low.
- **Client readiness** — from his Case Camp material: communicating ideas clearly, providing concise answers about complex issues, conversing naturally, and devising a new communication strategy when the first one is not landing.

## Factor 3 — User-specific

Read his stated focus areas and the accumulated weakness record. Then:

1. **Name every intersection explicitly.** If the performance touched something he is actively working on — well or badly — say so by name and quote the moment.
2. **Report the direction.** Better, worse, or unchanged since the last observation, with the evidence count behind that claim.
3. **Render this section even when empty.** If no focus areas are set, say *"No focus areas recorded — this section is unpopulated, not clean."* Silently omitting it makes an unconfigured system look like a passing grade.

**Thresholds:** 2 sightings of a recurring issue → watch list. 3 → promoted to the weakness record and it drives drills.

---

## Feedback unit — seven parts, none optional

1. **Observation** — exact and neutral. Quote it, timestamp it if you have one.
2. **Impact** — what it did to the analysis or to the interviewer's experience.
3. **Standard** — what effective performance looks like here.
4. **Root-cause hypothesis** — labelled explicitly as an inference, with confidence.
5. **Replacement behaviour** — concrete, repeatable, sayable in one sentence.
6. **Drill** — short, with a success criterion.
7. **Retest** — when and how you will check it transferred.

Skipping 4–7 is the most common coaching failure. A list of observations with no
next action is a critique, not coaching.

## Output template

Render as three clearly separated blocks so any one can be suppressed or reweighted:

```markdown
## Case: {title} — {date}
Overall Performance: {Bad|Fine|Good|Great|Perfect}   ← Daniel's scale, no other scale
Confidence: {low|medium|high}

### 1 · Baseline
{per stage: met / not met, with the quoted evidence for anything not met}

### 2 · Good → Great
{gradations, each with the specific moment that earned it}

### 3 · Focus areas
{named intersections — or the explicit "unpopulated" statement}

### The one thing
{single highest-leverage constraint + the seven-part feedback unit}
```

Use `Overall Performance` on Daniel's existing scale — **Bad · Fine · Good · Great · Perfect**. Do not invent a parallel scale, and do not emit a numeric or composite score under any circumstances.

## Pitfalls

- **Never emit a composite score.** No "7/10", no "82% ready". You have no calibration to real interview decisions and a number implies one.
- **Never blend the three factors.** A great elevation moment does not offset a missed baseline item, and merging them hides which is which.
- **One bottleneck, not twenty comments.** Name the one or two constraints that explain most of the downstream problems and explicitly park the rest.
- **Do not invent frontmatter.** `[BLANK]` — the case-note schema has no slot for per-factor scoring. Hold it in the scoring output and the worklog entry until Daniel decides where it lives.
- **His material outranks any general rubric.** Where a study guide and an outside source disagree, his wins, and you say which you used.
